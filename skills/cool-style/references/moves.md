# Moves

A move is a decision you can name. These are the ones the studied sites make, grouped by what they act on. Pick **one signature move** and carry it all the way; add **two or three supporting moves**; leave the rest plain. Each entry: what it is · where it was seen (#n, see `data/sites.json` or `scripts/examples.mjs`) · how · the guardrail. Code for the starred (☆) ones is in `recipes.md`.

## A. The container

1. **The subject's shape is the layout.** Find the object your content already is and build it in HTML: a spreadsheet for an essay about datasets (#481), a 1984 Mac window for a history of interfaces (#501), a calendar for a daily comic's archive (#21), a carpet woven in ASCII around a poem about a carpet (#42), a ring for a game of telephone (#203). *Guard:* the real text must stay real text (selectable, readable, in order).
2. **Clone a trusted template, then let your subject corrupt it.** fungal.page (#320) copies a Wikipedia article pixel for pixel, then a variable font's "grow" axis slowly sprouts mycelium out of the sentences. The familiar host makes the intrusion legible.
3. **One engine, many costumes.** The same Are.na channel data rendered as a Win95 desktop, a scrapbook and newspaper classifieds (#61, #55, #58). Separate content from skin, then make the skin a metaphor.
4. **Build the metaphor from divs, not images.** A signpost from six divs and `perspective` (#197); a pebble cairn from box-shadows (#436); a leaf from one div with `border-radius: 100% 0` (#874). It stays sharp, light and editable.
5. **The page performs its thesis.** Plain HTML for an essay about plain HTML (#146); a 10,000-item list that is mostly "Coming Soon" (#149); a solar site that goes offline when the battery is empty (#34).
6. **Make the landing page a live instance of the product.** Moving Parts (#701) pitches with its own chat bubble and input; #811's homepage is the working tool.
7. **Turn a system primitive into material.** 190 loading spinners at random phases (#622); native OS buttons as the words of a poem (#463); form widgets as pixels (#442); nested 1px frames as the whole identity (#909).

## B. Navigation

8. **The sentence is the nav.** Write the description; link its noun phrases; delete the menu (#316, #282, #530, #783). ☆ `sentence` starter.
9. **Navigation as furniture.** A bookshelf (#652), a signpost (#197), embossed pebbles (#436), a cairn, a town map (#657), ASCII parcels (#663), an onion you peel (#789), iPad widgets (#796).
10. **Nav in the four corners or on the edges.** Labels pinned to all four edges, sometimes rotated (#289, #284, #293); the content owns the middle.
11. **Name sections by something other than topic.** By tense — War / Ist / Kommt (#600); by verb; by sense (eyes / ears, #301); by count.
12. **Pictures as words.** Logos in place of names (#856), sketches in place of nouns (#547), thumbnails at line height inside headlines (#865).
13. **A random door.** A button that takes you somewhere unexpected (`atlas` starter); `/?random` replaying the homepage sentence by sentence (#720).
14. **Checkbox navigation.** Ticked items appear as layers on the same page (#726, #129).
15. **The sitemap as a tree.** Box-drawing characters: `├──`, `└──` (#232).
16. **Arrows that mean something.** ↗ for outbound, → for inbound (#816); parentheses around links (#140); a ⌕ before every link as an SEO joke made useful (#610).
17. **One subdomain per room.** image.elliott.computer, audio., video., play. — the domain is the map (elliott.computer; #9).

## C. Type

18. **The browser's own face, on purpose.** Times New Roman at 16px, default blue links, no stylesheet — 9% of live sites declare no face at all; many exemplars (#316, #125, #146, #608). Pick it because the subject is plain, archival, or about the web itself.
19. **One self-hosted face with a reason.** A foundry face the subject earns (Diatype, Neue Haas, Monument Grotesk, Söhne, a period serif). 69% self-host. → `type.md`
20. **Mono as the machine's voice.** Labels, dates, counts, metadata in monospace (55% of stacks) beside a grotesk or serif for everything human.
21. **Scale contrast, nothing in between.** One element at 110–300px, everything else 10–13px (#195, #201, #599). A third of sites use four sizes or fewer.
22. **Type as image.** Cropped at the viewport edge, hollow, pinned to the bottom, dithered inside outlines (#544), a letter as its Bézier skeleton (#776).
23. **Fit the word to the screen.** ☆ §17. A name exactly as wide as the window (#7, #850).
24. **Two faces inside one sentence.** Grotesk facts, italic serif titles, like a concert programme (#353, #911, #912).
25. **Each name in its own face.** Font lists where every entry is set in itself (#352, #75); a different display face per card (#748).
26. **Type that behaves like the subject.** Letters on waves for a space agency (#347, #638); spacing from wind data (#669) ☆ §28; a variable axis keyframed to "grow" (#320); a word ring rewriting itself letter by letter (#838).
27. **Glyphs as ornament and UI.** ✿ ※ ☾ ⌖ → ⁂ as bullets, favicons and buttons; buttons as punctuation in `((( MINT )))` (#335); a page border built from a pixel font's glyphs (#805).
28. **ASCII as illustration.** A laptop drawn in characters (#232), box scores in pipes and plus signs (#288), thumbnails rendered to ASCII (#447). ☆ §13.
29. **Vertical and rotated labels.** Metadata rotated into the margin like a binder spine (#153, #10). ☆ §14.

## D. Colour

30. **One ink on one ground.** Type, rules and images in a single colour: ultramarine (#157), vermilion on grey (#551), red on orange (#794), green on green (#742), white on `#0000ff` (#776, #676). → `colour.md`
31. **Take the accent from the subject.** The red of the archived book's jacket (#3); the green sampled from the artwork (#587); a lilac ramp named after the metaphor (#128).
32. **The work supplies the colour.** Monochrome interface; the photographs, covers and swatches are the only colour on the page (#104, #209, #251).
33. **A saturated field.** Pure primaries are the most-used non-greys in the canon (`#ffff00`, `#ff0000`, `#0000ff`, `#00ff00`). Acid lime with black was the 2020 accent (#775, #781, #802).
34. **Tonal zoning.** Two adjacent hues do all the separation — red type on orange, a red rule, a gradient spine (#829) — instead of boxes.
35. **Duotone everything.** Tint every image to the ink (#157, #173, #736) or multiply every scan into the paper colour so mismatched sources read as one collection (#892).
36. **Colour that changes for a reason.** The hour (`poster` starter), the weather (#832), the season, the site's "energy" (#283), the issue (#630).
37. **Difference blend.** Text that recolours itself against what it sits on (#498, #843, #311). ☆ §12.
38. **Default link blue as a quotation.** `#0000ee` and `#551a8b` — instantly "the web".

## E. Images

39. **Uncropped and catalogued.** Same-scale cut-outs (#251), numbered captions ("Img. 02 of 44", #685), every item in the same frame.
40. **Dither to two colours.** Cheaper, and a look (#34, #76, #685). ☆ §22.
41. **Objects floating on a field.** Cut-out stones, chairs, clothes, 3D chrome, against flat colour or sky (#94, #503, #793).
42. **The whole collection at once.** A colour cell per item that resolves into its image (#439); a mosaic; a ring (#203).
43. **Sky as ground.** Clouds and Windows XP's Bliss recur as backgrounds (#306, #463, #811, #907): the web's default landscape.
44. **Blur as a state.** Sold-out items blurred, not removed (#494); hover to unblur (#704); blur-up placeholders so 400 images feel instant (#522).
45. **Show provenance.** Every GIF links back to its GeoCities source (#586); scans credit their archive; file weight beside each image (#166).

## F. Structure and detail

46. **Rules, not boxes.** Hairlines, dotted borders (#507: one dotted border for everything), 4px black rules between ticker rows (#865).
47. **Fixed slots.** Every card or row has tag, image, title, domain, date in the same place (#479, #473).
48. **Counts on everything.** "1,504 marks · 725 designers · 30 countries" (#3); "8 lines, 40,320 poems" (#778).
49. **Machine values as ornament.** Hex line numbers, Unix timestamps (#447), scroll distance in px as page numbers (#703), coordinates as the title (#543).
50. **Nested frames.** The same 1px border-plus-padding wrapper nested a dozen deep (#909).
51. **Concave corners and odd shapes.** Panels that curve inward where they meet (#108), notched layouts (#420), per-item masks — square, circle, arch (#549).

## G. Interaction

52. **Hover a name, see the work.** ☆ §1. Also on focus.
53. **Things you can pick up.** ☆ §10. Draggable GIFs (#277), cards, windows; they remember where you left them.
54. **One toy-like control over a strict grid.** A physics ball pit as the filter (#479); everything else disciplined.
55. **Drag a lens.** The specimen or image seen through a window you move (#254).
56. **Pointer as a parameter.** x and y bound to two font axes, with live values in the corners (#615).
57. **Reveal by touching.** Tiles that clear to show a hidden poem (#228); words that fade until hovered (#360).
58. **A custom cursor with a job.** Prev/next cursors on slideshow halves; a single blue dot plus a difference-blend headline (#843). ☆ §2–3.
59. **Sound, on request.** Sound consent as the opening gesture (#452); a "with / without sound" gate (#237). ☆ §11.
60. **Keyboard as instrument.** WASD to fly between documents (#578); arrow keys to step a dataset (#669).
61. **Chance.** A different arrangement, greeting or image every visit; a "re-scatter" button (#649). ☆ §9.

## H. Time and liveness

62. **The page knows the time — with a reason.** The studio's city clock; today's date in the masthead (#239); a colophon stamped with the visitor's load time (#431). ☆ §18.
63. **Opening hours.** Closed at night, with a note box for the morning (#828).
64. **Idle states.** Stop moving and a square sun sets (#243); a screensaver; "the site went dark to save energy" (#747).
65. **Live data as material.** Weather, wind, moon phase, trains, a webcam (#12, #51, #669, #678, #832). ☆ §28.
66. **The site reports on itself.** Its weight, its battery, the visitor's screen size (#34, #678). ☆ §29.
67. **Age and neglect.** A birth date and an "energy" that changes the stylesheet; entries fade if not visited (#283); "(active)" and "(dusty)" links (#725).
68. **A living tab.** Title as a ticker (#52, #271); "come back" when hidden. ☆ §5.

## I. Voice

69. **Say exactly what it is.** "An infinite I-Spy of Wikipedia." "Every train, a note." Titles are short (median 18 characters) and specific. → `voice.md`
70. **Instructions as the copy.** A tired one-line brief that hands the decorating to the visitor (#277); the only instruction run as a header marquee (#700).
71. **Honesty as charm.** A headline warning that the page is slow (#504); a FAQ admitting the site's own font was bought (#458); strike-through as edit history (#483, #828).
72. **Rename the commerce.** The cart renamed a to-do list (#635, #565); a joke MSRP (#631).

## J. Details

73. **A favicon of its own.** A glyph in an SVG data URI; a favicon used as a canvas (#215). ☆ §6.
74. **The cookie banner in character.** Clippy asking about cookies in a Windows 95 dialog (#179); the consent as the front door (#281); a bitmap speech bubble (#488).
75. **A loader that's part of the work.** A title card that just says the name and that it's loading (#290); a cream starburst (#461); an XP-style level bar (#596). Never a spinner over an empty page.
76. **A 404 in character.** A localStorage notepad presented as the back page of the site (#898).
77. **The inspector is a room.** HTML comments in a character's voice (#283), a console essay (#267). ☆ §21.
78. **A colophon.** What it's set in, built with, hosted on, weighs. Edition numbers (#431).
79. **Accessibility in the nav.** "Increase Contrast" and "Increase Text Size" beside About (#102); an "Effects Off" switch beside the theme toggle (#292); a visible Motion on/off (#472). The canon's best answer to its own blind spot.

## K. Participation

80. **One line each.** Visitors complete a haiku line by line (#654); add a room (#423); stamp a flower and photograph it (#410).
81. **Presence.** Anonymous "adjective-animal" visitors and "left the site" (#95); creatures with hidden cursors (#324).
82. **A guestbook that's an object.** Stationery (#92); a note box on the closed sign (#828).

---

**Combining.** A strong site is usually one move from A or B, one from C or D, one from G or H, and impeccable F and J. The exemplars' signature moves are mostly containers and sentences (A, B, I) rather than effects (G): an effect alone rarely made a site memorable.
