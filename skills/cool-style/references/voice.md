# Voice

Half of what makes the studied sites feel authored is the words. The examples below are written for this guide in the manner of the sites, not copied from them.

## What the numbers say

- **Titles are short.** Median `<title>` length across 724 live pages: 18 characters. 11% are entirely lowercase, 7% entirely caps; separators are `|` (95), `-` (71), `—` (31), `–` (30), `·` (8).
- **Brochure language is almost absent.** "Get started"-style calls to action on 3% of pages, verbs like elevate / unlock / seamless / empower on 5%, a `.hero` section on 6%.
- **Lowercase is a register, not a rule.** About 1 in 20 sites run almost entirely lowercase, about 1 in 20 almost entirely caps. Pick one for the chrome and hold it.
- **They link out.** A quarter carry 10+ outbound links on the first page.

## Ten habits

1. **Say exactly what it is, in the fewest words.** "A log of every song one microphone heard." "Maps of places that no longer exist." "Notes kept while building a radio." The best titles describe; they don't promise.
2. **The name can be the sentence.** Site titles that are whole statements — *Every train, a note.*, *Watching Grass Grow*, *Rotating Sandwiches* — tell you the idea before the page loads.
3. **Write like one person talking to one person.** First person, present tense, specific: "I'm drawing a chair a day until I run out of chairs." Not "We craft meaningful experiences."
4. **Instructions are copy.** When the page does something, say what to do in one plain line: "drag anything", "click the sun", "type a word and wait". A sentence that hands the work to the visitor can be the whole concept (#277).
5. **Be honest about the site itself.** How heavy it is, how long it takes, that it goes offline when the battery's low (#34), that the font was bought (#458), that the shop is closed. Honesty reads as confidence.
6. **Keep the seams.** Strike-through for corrections ("open until ~~7pm~~ 11pm"); "(dusty)" next to old links; dead links struck, not deleted (#906). Edit history is texture.
7. **Rename the machinery.** A cart called a to-do list (#635), a cookie banner spoken by a period character (#179), a 404 that is a notepad (#898), a loader that is a title card (#290). Every UI noun is a chance to stay in character.
8. **Numbers are writing.** "1,504 marks", "day 303 of 365", "38 KB", "8 lines, 40,320 poems" (#778). A count is more vivid than an adjective.
9. **Quote and credit.** Sources named, archives credited, friends linked. A links page is a portrait of the author.
10. **Humour is deadpan.** A joke price, a solemn scout oath for a rave collective (#628), a perfume-ad register for a residency (#620). Never winking emoji; let the format carry it.

## Parts of a page, written

| part | instead of | write |
|---|---|---|
| `<title>` | "Home \| Studio Name — Creative Agency" | "Studio Name, who make signs for small museums" |
| first line | "Welcome to my website!" | what it is, one sentence, as you'd say it aloud |
| about | a résumé in third person | two plain paragraphs, first person, one specific detail nobody else would include |
| nav | Home / About / Work / Contact | the sentence (`sentence` starter), or words in the site's metaphor: shelf / drawer / door |
| link labels | "Learn more", "Click here" | the destination: "the 40 postcards", "write to me" |
| empty state | "No items found" | "Nothing written yet." / "Not yet." |
| error | "Something went wrong" | what happened and what to do, in the same voice |
| closed / offline | an error page | a sign: hours, a reason, a way to leave a note |
| footer | © 2026 All rights reserved | a colophon: set in, built with, hosted on, weighs, last updated |
| buttons | "Submit" | the verb that happens: "send the letter", "add your line" |

## Colophon template

> Kept by *your name* in *a place*. Set in *face* and the system mono. Hand-coded; no tracking; about *N* KB. Hosted on *host*. Last updated *date*. Links out to *some friends*.

## Domain names

About 1 in 6 studied sites use an expressive TLD — `.world` (12), `.studio` (12), `.online` (10), `.site` (9), `.design` (8), `.work` (8), `.computer` (7), `.website` (7), `.art` (7), `.earth` (6), `.club` (6), `.space` (6), `.page` (5), `.fun`, `.land`, `.exposed`, `.zone`, `.cafe`, `.town`, `.garden`, `.rest`. The domain is the first line of copy: `gossips.cafe`, `url.town`, `fungal.page`, `watching-grass-grow.com`, `entrances2hell.co.uk`. One subdomain per room is a voice too (`image.`, `audio.`, `play.`).

## Never

Brochure verbs (elevate, unlock, seamless, empower, supercharge, revolutionise, next-gen, cutting-edge, best-in-class); "Welcome to my website"; lorem ipsum or "Your Name Here" left in; emoji used as bullet points for features; exclamation marks doing the work of a sentence; copy that would fit any other site.
